IF YOU READ ONLY ONE THING

  ON YOUR PHONE, AS AN APP  (about four minutes, and the best way)
  Put it online once — instructions below — then open the address on
  your phone and choose "Add to Home Screen". You get a proper app
  icon. It opens with no signal. It survives being in the yard.

  ON YOUR COMPUTER, RIGHT NOW
  Open a terminal in this folder and type:  npm start
  Then go to http://localhost:5173

  WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY YOU CANNOT JUST DOUBLE-CLICK index.html ANY MORE
  v10 is built from many small files instead of one enormous one, which
  is what makes it possible to keep adding to. Browsers refuse to load
  files that way when you open them off the disk directly — a security
  rule, not a bug, and there is no way round it.

  You have not lost anything. Type:  npm run bake
  That produces  dist/almanac.html  — ONE file, everything inside it,
  which you can double-click, email to yourself, or carry on a USB
  stick. It works with no internet and no server, exactly like v9 did.
  Add  -- --with-state backup.json  to bake your logged data in too.

WHAT CHANGED IN v10

  A DAILY ACTION DESK
  One screen that answers "what do I do in the next hour", ordered by
  what actually breaks if you skip it, with the reason attached to every
  line. Everything else in the app is reference material for this page.

  THE ORCHARD IS NOW ON A CLOCK
  Each of the eight trees carries its whole schedule: which bag it is
  standing in, when it outgrows it, when the root-prune falls due once
  it stops climbing, and how far through the wait for first fruit it is.
  Miss a step-up and the roots circle and girdle the trunk. Miss a
  root-prune in the final bag and it strangles itself over three years.
  Neither failure shows above ground — which is the whole reason this
  screen exists.

  SEEDS ARE TRACKED PROPERLY
  One record per SOWING, not per variety, so re-sowing radish every
  fortnight from October is tracked instead of overwritten. Germination
  is worked out as a temperature budget rather than a number of days: a
  chilli seed needs the same accumulated warmth whether it gets it in
  six days at 30 °C or eighteen at 18 °C. Below its base temperature the
  app tells you plainly that nothing will happen and that the seed is
  fine — which is what actually went wrong with a January sowing.
  The hardening-off ladder is written for a 45 °C site: morning sun
  only, never afternoon.

  FOUR THEMES INSTEAD OF TWO
  Two looks, each with light and dark:
    Jungle     — lush and editorial. Serif headings, soft shadows,
                 canopy light. What v9 looked like, done properly.
    Precision  — an instrument panel. Sharp corners, hairline rules,
                 a faint blueprint grid, denser data.
  Press  y  to swap look,  t  to cycle light / dark / follow-the-phone.

  THE APP NOW SHOWS ITS WORKING
  Every temperature is labelled MODELLED or MEASURED. Log eight
  afternoon readings in a zone and that zone stops being a guess and
  starts using your numbers. There is a progress bar for it.

  THINGS IT CAN NOW WORK OUT RATHER THAN ASSERT
    · The June sun here reaches 87.5° — within two and a half degrees
      of straight overhead. That one fact is why an overhang shades the
      south balcony all summer and floods it in December, and the app
      now calculates the whole year of it from your overhang depth.
    · Chill hours: zero. That is why apple, litchi and peach are out.
    · Root-zone temperature. A black plastic pot in Zone A in May
      reaches 54 °C. A pale fabric bag under 50% net with two inches of
      mulch reaches 44.5 °C. Feeder roots die from about 40 °C, and the
      leaves look perfectly fine while it happens.

  TWO REAL ERRORS FIXED
    · Heat index was badly overstated, because a monthly AVERAGE
      humidity was being paired with the day's HIGH temperature. Air
      holds about twice as much water at 40 °C as at 30 °C, so the
      afternoon figure is much lower. The balcony was reading 60 °C in
      August. It now reads 49 °C — still serious, no longer crying wolf.
    · The fertiliser calculator was prescribing a salt dose. Labels say
      "2–3 grams per litre", which is a strength, not an amount. Taken
      literally across a proper soak of a 43-litre bag that is 32 grams
      of 19:19:19 in one go. Fertiliser is now measured per ten litres
      of soil, which is how it is actually done.

  ALSO
    · Ctrl+K searches everything from anywhere.
    · Deep links: you can send someone #orchard/mango.
    · Tighter security headers — the old build had to allow inline
      scripts, this one does not.
    · Five plants appeared twice in the catalogue with the same internal
      name. Harmless before, not harmless now that the app refers to
      plants by name. Fixed and labelled.

PUTTING IT ON YOUR PHONE — about four minutes

  1. Go to  netlify.com/drop  in a browser.
  2. Drag this whole folder onto that page. Wait for it to finish.
     It gives you an address like  something-random.netlify.app
  3. Click "Site configuration", then "Environment variables",
     then "Add a variable".
         Name:   LEDGER_KEY
         Value:  any password you will remember
     This is the only lock on your data. Without it, anyone who guesses
     the address can read and change your ledger.
  4. Click "Deploys", then "Trigger deploy", then "Deploy site".
     (The password only takes effect after this step. Do not skip it.)
  5. Open the address on your phone.
       iPhone:  Share button, then "Add to Home Screen"
       Android: three-dot menu, then "Install app"
  6. In the app, go to Settings, type the same password, press Connect.
     Do this on every device you use.

  Same password everywhere, or your phone and laptop will not see each
  other's changes.


COMING FROM v8 OR v9?
  Nothing to do. Open v10 once and your pots, watering history, buy
  list and notes are carried across automatically the first time it
  loads. The old data is left where it was as a backup and is never
  written to again.

  One thing worth knowing: v9 referred to plants by their POSITION in
  the catalogue. v10 refers to them by name. The changeover happens
  once, on that first load, while the old ordering still applies —
  so open v10 before making any further edits in an old copy.


WHERE YOUR DATA LIVES
  · In whichever browser you used, always.
  · In your Netlify site, if you connected it.
  · In Settings → "Download backup", which saves everything including
    photos as one file. Keep one somewhere. Do this before any big
    change.


THE FILES — what each one is, so nothing looks alarming

  index.html            the page itself. Small now — it is only a frame.
  src/                  the app, in small files.
     core/              storage, syncing, routing — the plumbing
     engine/            the gardening knowledge, as arithmetic
     ui/                themes, search, shared pieces
     views/             one file per screen
     styles/            colours, type and layout
  data/                 the catalogue and all the reference tables
  tools/                helper scripts (see below)
  legacy/index-v9.html  the previous version, kept whole
  sw.js                 makes it work with no signal
  netlify/functions/    the piece that syncs between your devices
  docs/BLUEPRINT.md     why it is built this way. Worth reading once.

  You never need to edit any of them.

WHAT TO DO FIRST, THIS WEEK

  Outdoors — this is time-critical. Build and cure the two soil mixes
  now. The coco peat must be soaked in calcium nitrate first or it will
  quietly starve your plants of calcium. The mix then needs five to
  seven days covered in the shade before anything goes into it. Pot up
  the eight plants in early September. Feed nothing until about 1
  October.

  Indoors — buy the rack and the grow light before buying another
  plant. Half the catalogue cannot survive that room on window light
  alone.

  And do the crush-and-smell test on the three tall plants. It takes
  two seconds and it is still marked unresolved. If they are ginger
  they want double the water and much more light, and they die back
  completely around December — do not throw the pots out.

  Then buy two cheap hygrometers, one for Zone C and one for Zone A,
  and log an afternoon reading whenever you walk past. Eight of them
  and the app stops guessing about that zone. It is the cheapest
  upgrade available to you.


May in that room models to around 50°C against a 45.3°C outdoor
average. March through June is the hard part, not winter. Plan for
shade.
